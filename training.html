Permanent weekly programme

Build the
fight-ready body.

This is one stable programme, not a series of blocks. Boxing remains the priority; Tuesday, Thursday and Saturday build the strength, power, speed, conditioning, shoulder endurance and mobility needed to stay sharp across all three 2-minute rounds.

Competition target: January 2027 amateur bout · 3 × 2 min. Bodyweight target: walk around roughly 75–77 kg and avoid relying on a major acute weight cut. Primary performance standard: round 3 should still have moving legs, fast hands, a functional guard, controlled breathing and clear decisions.
Weekly rhythm

Six training days. Sunday off.

Supplementary sessions never replace boxing. Friday sparring and coach feedback remain the most specific preparation.

Tuesday

Strength + power.

Power work comes first while fresh. Main lifts stay strong without taking you to failure.

Power & speed

Box jumps4 × 3
Rotational med-ball throws4 × 4/side
Accelerations6 × 20 m

Rest 60–120 seconds as needed. Every rep should be explosive and technically clean. Do not turn plyometrics into conditioning.

Strength

Trap-bar deadlift3 × 3
Bench press3 × 5
Bulgarian split squat3 × 6/leg
Pull-ups3 × 6

Keep roughly 1–2 reps in reserve. Add weight only when all prescribed reps are clean.

Shoulder support

Face pulls
2 × 15–20
Band/cable external rotations
2 × 15–20/side

These support scapular control and shoulder health without creating a large extra fatigue load.

Thursday

Speed + fight conditioning.

This is the main supplementary conditioning session. The aim is usable output, not exhaustion for its own sake.

Acceleration

15 m accelerations6 reps
30 m accelerations4 reps

60–120 seconds recovery. Stop chasing speed when mechanics deteriorate.

Boxing-specific conditioning

Work4 × 2 min
Rest1 min

Each 2-minute round: repeat 20 sec high-output punching + 20 sec movement/technical work three times. During movement periods, keep the hands active: guard, footwork, defence, feints and resets. After the fourth round, recover fully.

Four rounds deliberately exceed the competition requirement of three. Quality matters more than adding more rounds.

Zone 230 min

Band punches are fast but controlled. Keep the hands returning to guard; do not chase failure.

Friday sparring rule: if Friday is a hard sparring session, reduce Saturday volume. Do not force the full programme at the expense of boxing recovery.
Progression

Same exercises. Better execution.

The programme changes materially only when fight camp begins. Until then, progress inside the same weekly structure.

Strength

Start around RPE 6–7. Build toward RPE 7–8. When every set and rep is clean with 1–2 reps in reserve, add a small amount of weight and build back up.

Power

Increase jump height, throw quality or sprint quality gradually. Do not add reps just to make the session harder. Stop when speed or landing quality drops.

Conditioning

The key measure is the quality of round 3 versus round 1: legs, hands, shoulders, breathing and decision-making should remain usable.

Return from layoff

Earn the volume.

You are returning after a long period away from regular boxing training, so the first few weeks are about re-acclimatisation.

  • Keep the prescribed structure, but start loads and explosive work conservatively.
  • Let the coach control sparring intensity, especially early in the return.
  • Do not add extra conditioning because a session feels easy.
  • Persistent pain, unusual fatigue or a clear drop in boxing performance is a reason to reduce load and seek appropriate professional advice.
